The Workbench

Seven plans that might actually work

They are written on cards the way they were written down, which is not in any particular order. Two of them are finished. Three are on the bench. The last two are still only a heading, which is a respectable place for a plan to be.

Plan one

The clock that tells the time in songs

The hands still move and they are still right, but at the top of every hour it plays the chorus of a winter song instead of chiming. Half past, it plays nothing. On purpose.

Parts: a music box, a stepper motor, one very patient spring.

In progress
Plan two

The window that remembers rain

A pane of glass that keeps the shape of every raindrop that has ever landed on it, and shows them back to you when the weather turns dry.

Parts: oiled paper, a length of wire, patience.

Not started
Plan three

A shelf for letters unsent

A small pine shelf by the door where the letters you never posted wait until they can be. There is a rule about how long. I have not decided the rule.

Parts: pine, two brass hooks, one stubborn drawer.

In progress
Plan four

The kettle that hums a quarter-tone off

It boils correctly. It also hums slightly wrong, and no amount of tuning has fixed it, so it is now allowed to. It is the best thing in the flat.

Parts: a kettle, a filing.

Finished
Plan five

A bell for whoever is nearly home

It rings when the streetlight outside comes on, so you know somebody is five minutes away and can put the kettle on before they arrive.

Parts: a photocell, a bicycle bell, a very long wire.

Not started
Plan six

The tree that keeps its own calendar

Each branch lights up once, one branch per day, from the first of December to Christmas Eve. The last bulb is the only one you have to plug in yourself.

Parts: twenty-four bulbs, a clock, a tree.

Finished
Plan seven

A map of every bird that hit the window

Drawn in soft pencil on the inside of the glass: one bird per collision, with the date and the time written underneath. The window is filling up.

Parts: a pencil, a sharp eye, window cleaner.

In progress

New plans are written on the back of old ones, which is why none of them have a date.
